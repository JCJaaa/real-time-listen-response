## Core Argument
- **Topic**: Leadership in South Korea's Doctors' Strike: Solutions and limitations in a complex healthcare environment, 2024–2026.
- **Main thesis**: Leadership (evidence-based management, distributed leadership, negotiation) moved the 2024–2026 crisis from top-down conflict toward dialogue and evidence-informed decisions, **but leadership alone cannot fix structural problems** like low pay and heavy workload in essential care.

## Key Numbers & Citations (Use These Exact Figures)
- **Doctor density**: `2.7` per 1,000 in South Korea (including Korean medicine doctors) vs `3.9` OECD average (OECD, 2025, cited in Jo, 2025). Outpatient visits: `18` per person/year, highest in OECD (Jo, 2025).
- **Regional gap**: Seoul `3.61` vs North Gyeongsang `1.41` per 1,000 — about `2.6x` (Yoon, 2025, Table 2).
- **Specialty gap (2024 residency fill rates)**: Paediatrics `25.9%`, OB/GYN `67.4%`, Emergency `79.6%` vs Ophthalmology `172.6%`, Plastic Surgery `165.8%`, Dermatology `143.1%` (Lim, 2023).
- **2024 Walkout timeline**:
  - Feb 6, 2024: Gov't announced `+2,000` medical school places (`3,058 -> 5,058`, `+65%`) (MOHW, 2024).
  - Feb 19, 2024: Interns & residents began resigning; gov't issued back-to-work orders & threatened licence suspensions (Oh and Kim, 2024).
  - May 2024: Actual 2025 expansion reduced to `~1,500` (Park, B., 2024).
  - July 2024: Licence suspension plan dropped (Park, J.-M., 2024).
  - April 2025: 2026 quota returned to `3,058` (KBS World, 2025; Yi, 2025).
  - Aug–Sep 2025: Trainees returned after `~18 months` (Choi, 2025; KBS World, 2025).
- **Consequences**:
  - Emergency visits: `-24,871` visits/week immediate drop (Han, 2025).
  - Cancer surgery: `20%–32%` immediate drop for 5 of 6 cancers at tertiary hospitals (Son, Jung and Joo, 2026).
  - Mortality (mixed): No increase in national 2024 mortality (Kim and Kim, 2025), but `+0.39 percentage points` in 30-day post-hospitalisation mortality across 200 teaching hospitals (Kim, D.D. et al., 2026).
- **2025–2026 Policy Response & 3 Theoretical Lenses**:
  1. **Evidence-based management** (Walsh, 2016: theoretical, empirical, experiential evidence): 2024 `2,000` figure was top-down without capacity/stakeholder evidence; 2025–26 Physician Workforce Supply and Demand Estimation Committee projected `~668` extra places/year for 2027–31. Limitation: model assumptions uncertain; medical school capacity still needs assessment. (If asked why 3 types instead of Barends, Rousseau & Briner 2014's 4 types: we followed Walsh 2016, same core point that stakeholder/practitioner evidence was missing.)
  2. **Distributed leadership** (Gronn, 2002): 2024 gov't held formal power alone; 2026 had `7 rounds` of public policy discussions (Jung and Kim, 2026) and formal stakeholder routes (Yonhap, 2025). Limitation: consultation != shared leadership; doctors' association president walked out before the final vote.
  3. **Conflict management & negotiation** (Bauer and Erdogan, 2012 — 5 phases: Investigate, BATNA, Present, Bargain, Close): 18-month disruption worsened both sides' BATNA; 2026 plan sets `3,342` extra places across 2027–31 via targeted regional doctor track (Lee, 2026). Note: Thailand evidence shows `29%` rural retention in targeted training vs `18%` standard track (Pagaiya, Kongkam and Sriratana, 2015).
- **Recommendations (Slide 9)**:
  1. **Talk first** (involve doctors before setting targets).
  2. **Be transparent** (share model data/assumptions and update regularly).
  3. **Fix working conditions** (improve pay & workload in essential care, track regional retention).
